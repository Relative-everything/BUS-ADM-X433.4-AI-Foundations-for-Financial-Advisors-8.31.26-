Margaret "Meg" Cole, 64President and CEO, Cole Precision Components Inc. Bought all 1,000 CPC shares from her father Walter Hensley on 1 July 2016 for $14,000,000; that purchase price is her basis. She did not found the company and did not inherit the stock.
David Cole, 63Hospital pharmacist, retired 2024. Social Security eligible, not claimed. Married 34 years. Both spouses are Illinois-domiciled for life, so the estate is exposed to the Illinois estate tax above $4,000,000, which is not indexed and not portable.
ChildrenClaire, 34, physician, financially independent, wants no ownership role. Nathan, 31, works in CPC operations and has not been told a competitor made an unsolicited inquiry in October 2025.
CPCCole Precision Components Inc., a C corporation in Rockford, Illinois, 180 employees, precision aerospace fasteners. Revenue $42,400,000, EBITDA $9,170,000. Pays $5,000,000 a year in dividends, a policy adopted in 2016 to service a note that was satisfied in 2025 and never revisited.
Balance sheetCPC stock $55,000,000 · taxable brokerage $2,500,000, of which 62% ($1,550,000) sits in three technology names · Meg IRA $3,000,000 · Meg inherited IRA $1,000,000 · David 403(b) $1,000,000 · residence $2,000,000 · cash $1,500,000 · T-bills $1,000,000. Total $67,000,000, no liabilities. CPC alone is 82.1% of it.
Retirement clocksMeg's applicable age is 75; her first distribution year is 2037. The inherited IRA runs a separate ten-year clock to 31 December 2031 with annual RMDs, because Walter Hensley died after his required beginning date. The 2026 RMD is $43,290.

The proposed structure, in five lines. Nothing in it has been executed.

  1. Meg contributes all CPC stock to Cole Family Holdings LLC, recapitalized into 100 voting and 900 non-voting units of 1,000. Economic rights per unit are identical; only the vote differs.
  2. A 30% combined lack-of-control and lack-of-marketability discount is asserted on the non-voting units, putting them at $38,500 each against $55,000 for a voting unit. No appraiser has been engaged and the memorandum cites no study.
  3. She gifts 52 non-voting units, $2,002,000, to the Cole 2026 Irrevocable Trust, a SLAT for David. That consumes $2,002,000 of her $15,000,000 exclusion, leaving $12,998,000.
  4. She sells 520 more units, $20,020,000, to the same trust for a demand note at 3.82%, interest only, $764,764 a year. Seed is exactly 10.00% of the note. Rev. Rul. 85-13 disregards the sale for income tax.
  5. The trust applies dividends above note interest to buy Meg's remaining 328 units in annual tranches, reaching all 900 by the end of year 5 for $12,628,000 of cash.
Where it landsMeg after closing: $5,500,000 of voting units + $12,628,000 of non-voting + $20,020,000 note = $38,148,000, down $16,852,000 from $55,000,000. That reduction has two components: $11,440,000 of below-pro-rata transfer plus $5,412,000 of discount on the 328 units she kept. The trust holds $22,022,000 of units against the $20,020,000 note: net equity $2,002,000, equal to the seed gift exactly.
The cashThe LLC distributes pro rata, so Meg draws on all 428 units she holds after closing: $2,140,000 in year 1, not $500,000. In years 1 through 4 the entire $5,000,000 round-trips to her. From year 6 she holds only the voting block: $1,264,764 in against $1,786,850 of tax and household gap, a shortfall of $522,086 a year.
The burnWhile grantor-trust status holds, Meg reports 100% of the LLC's income whatever the trust owns. The tax she pays on the trust's share is a transfer of value to the trust that consumes no exemption and is not a gift (Rev. Rul. 2004-64). $822,250 in year 1, $1,293,750 a year at steady state, $30,876,930 cumulative by Meg's age 89. It is the mechanism, not a cost.
Documents2011 wills and a joint revocable trust with a credit-shelter formula written for a $5,000,000 exclusion and no Illinois QTIP direction · a 2014 buy-sell, never amended, whose formula produces about $18,000,000 against a $55,000,000 indicated value · a 2023 appraisal concluding $38,000,000 with no discount study · unexecuted 2026 drafts of the trust and note · no LLC agreement, no 2026 valuation, no life insurance, no Form 709 ever filed.
CharitableNo donor-advised fund has ever existed. $80,000 a year in direct cash to four public charities. Meg chairs the Rockford Workforce Alliance and intends a $5,000,000 lifetime endowment to it. Undecided, unpledged, and the board has not been told.

Open in the file, and answered by no session.

  • Which rate governs the note. §7872's blended annual rate at 3.82%, which the memorandum uses, or §1274's short-term AFR at 4.10% for a note issued for property. The difference is $56,056 of interest a year. No controlling authority resolves it.
  • Whether the voting block carries a premium. The memorandum values 100 units holding sole voting control at pro-rata value with no adjustment.
  • Whether Meg's retained non-voting units carry a discount while she controls the LLC through the voting block. $5,412,000 of the estate reduction depends on the answer.
  • Whether discretionary distributions survive §2036(a)(2). Mirowski turned on mandatory distributions; the draft operating agreement makes them discretionary.
  • Whether the Illinois exposure is addressed at all. Neither spouse has engaged with it, and the 2011 documents contain no Illinois QTIP direction.

Planning parallel — discussionEvery Monte Carlo engine you run is a model with knobs: return assumptions, inflation, sequence-of-returns treatment, mortality table. A 91% probability of success is not a measurement. It is the output of a structure someone chose, fitted to assumptions someone set.

The Task a Transformer Cannot Reliably Learn

Wolfram trains a small transformer on a language with exactly one rule: parentheses must balance. It learns it well enough to look right, then fails.

From the assigned reading — his figuresWith 2 attention heads the learning converges after "10 million or so examples." Yet with its "400,000 or so laboriously trained weights," it still assigns a 15% probability to a closing parenthesis that would leave the sequence unbalanced. And: "even the full current ChatGPT has a hard time correctly matching parentheses in long sequences."
Sequence builder — the simulated model predicts what comes next
depth 0

Simulated shallow model — confidence degrades with depth, as Wolfram's trained net does

Counting is algorithmic. The network is feed-forward — "there's no looping or 'going back'" — so it approximates a counter from local pattern, and the approximation decays with depth.

Anything requiring an exact count across a long document is the failure case, not the edge case.

Where this bites in the Cole file — discussionEvery one of these is a counting or reconciliation task inside a long sequence, and every one is a place a fluent answer is least trustworthy:
  • Confirming the 2014 buy-sell formula value, roughly $18,000,000, against the $55,000,000 indicated value, and listing every provision that conflicts
  • Checking that the note principal on the draft instrument is $20,020,000 and that it is the same figure on every schedule and exhibit
  • Verifying the 30% discount is applied to the $49,500,000 pro-rata value of the 900 non-voting units, giving $34,650,000 and $38,500 a unit, and not to some other base, in each place it appears
  • Tracing whether the 2011 powers of attorney were superseded by anything in the 2016 revocable trust restatement
Ask the room: which of these have you already delegated to a tool this year, and did you total the numbers yourself?

The planning version — discussionAsk any frontier model what seed gift a sale to a grantor trust requires, and it will give you a percentage in a confident sentence. There Is No Separate AI Rulebook

FINRA's rules are technology-neutral. Regulatory Notice 24-09 creates no new obligations — it confirms the existing ones already reach this.

RuleAs applied to AI
Rule 3110 — SupervisionA reasonably designed system addressing technology governance, model risk management, data privacy and integrity, and model reliability and accuracy
Rule 2210 — CommunicationsContent standards apply to AI-generated content identically to human-generated content
Rules 3110.09, 4511; SEA 17a-4AI-generated correspondence must be retained
Third-party vendor useObligations apply whether the tool is built in-house or licensed

On 18 March 2024 the SEC settled against two advisers over AI claims: Delphia (USA) Inc., $225,000, for saying it used client data with machine learning when it had collected the data and never used it that way; and Global Predictions, Inc., $175,000, for advertising "expert AI driven forecasts" without substantiation. Both censured and ordered to cease and desist, under Advisers Act §206(2), §206(4) and Rules 206(4)-1 and 206(4)-7. Neither case was about a wrong answer. Both were about what the firm said it was doing.
