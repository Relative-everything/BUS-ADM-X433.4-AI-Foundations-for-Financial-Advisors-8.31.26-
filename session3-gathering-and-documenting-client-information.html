Gathering and Documenting Client Information

Grounding makes the model cite your sources. The nearest passage is not always the right one.

Session 1 established that the model predicts rather than knows. Session 2 established that it samples a plausible answer rather than computing the true one. Session 3 covers the standard remedy — hand the model your own documents and require it to answer from them — and measures how much of the problem that remedy actually removes.

The answer is: a large fraction, and not all of it. Four measurements set the boundary, and every one is on the board before the break. The mechanism underneath all four is the same mechanism Wolfram describes in the reading you did for tonight: text becomes an array of numbers, and "relevant" is operationalised as "close in that number space." Closeness is not correctness. Most of this session is spent on the gap between the two.

01 · Mechanism12 min

Embeddings and the Coordinates of Meaning Space

Wolfram's definition is the one to hold: an embedding is a way to represent the essence of something by an array of numbers, with the property that nearby things are represented by nearby numbers. A word embedding lays words out in a meaning space in which words nearby in meaning appear nearby in the space.

Two details from the reading matter more than the definition. The first is how the arrays are produced. Wolfram describes looking at large amounts of text — five billion words from the web in his example — and measuring how similar the environments are in which different words appear. Nothing in that procedure inspects meaning. It inspects company. The second is that the arrays are long: Wolfram gives 768 numbers per token for GPT-2 and 12,288 for GPT-3. Every two-dimensional picture of meaning space, his and the one below, is a projection of something with hundreds or thousands of axes down to two, and projection destroys information.

The map below is an illustrative hand-built layout — the positions were assigned, not learned. The arithmetic run on it is real: clicking a term computes Euclidean distance to every other term in the displayed coordinates and ranks them. That is the same operation a retriever performs, at two dimensions instead of a thousand.

02 · The failure this creates10 min

Distributional Similarity and Referential Difference

Wolfram's own worked example contains the failure mode. He explains that "alligator" and "crocodile" will often appear almost interchangeably in otherwise similar sentences, and that this is why they are placed near each other, while "turnip" and "eagle" do not appear in similar sentences and are placed far apart.

Read that as an engineer rather than as a reader. The property being measured is interchangeability of context. Alligators and crocodiles are different animals. The embedding does not encode that they are different; it encodes that people write about them the same way. For a general-purpose chatbot this is a harmless approximation. For a retriever pointed at a firm's document library it is the central hazard, because the documents that describe a firm's rules are written in near-identical language and differ in one governing condition.

Distributional proximity is the signal. Referential identity is what you needed.

03 · RAG, mechanically12 min

Retrieval Mechanics: Chunking, Indexing, and Ranking

Retrieval-augmented generation is four operations, none of them mysterious. Split the source documents into chunks. Convert each chunk into a vector. At query time, convert the query into a vector by the same procedure and rank the chunks by similarity. Paste the top-ranked chunks into the prompt and instruct the model to answer only from them.

The advisory use case is the one your firm already has: an internal library of standards, policies, model portfolios, meeting templates and prior plan documents that nobody can recall in full. The model is not being asked to know your firm. It is being asked to read the three paragraphs that the ranker handed it. Everything that can go wrong in the answer either went wrong in the ranking, or went wrong in the reading.

The retriever below is real, not a mock-up. It builds an inverse-document-frequency index over the ten chunks of a synthetic firm library, weights each term by tf × ln(1 + N/df), and ranks by cosine similarity. Two of the four preset queries are engineered to fail. The failure is not a bug in the code; it is the alligator-and-crocodile property, arriving in a client file.

04 · The engineering decision that breaks it10 min

Chunk Size and Condition Orphaning

Chunk size is the only parameter in a retrieval system that a non-engineer will ever be asked to choose, and it is the one that decides whether a rule arrives with the condition that governs it.

Anthropic's engineering write-up on contextual retrieval states the problem in one example: a chunk reads "the company's revenue grew by 3% over the previous quarter," and nothing in that chunk says which company or which quarter. The subject was two paragraphs earlier, in a different chunk. The embedding of the orphaned chunk carries none of it.

The advisory version is worse, because the orphaned element is usually not the subject but the condition. "The reserve is eighteen months of planned distributions" is a true sentence about retired households and a dangerous sentence about everyone else. Drag the slider and watch the condition separate from the number.

05 · Measurement10 min

Measured Retrieval Failure Rates

Anthropic measured the retrieval step alone, before any generation, as the share of queries for which the correct chunk was absent from the top twenty returned. The baseline was 5.7%. The best configuration reached 1.9%.

Two things are worth extracting from that pair of numbers. First, the direction and the size: adding context to each chunk before embedding, adding a lexical index alongside the vector index, and adding a reranking pass cut failures by two thirds. Second, the floor: 1.9% is not zero, and this is the retrieval step measured in isolation, on a benchmark built by the vendor of the technique. Whatever your production system does, it does not do better than the number the vendor published.

Anthropic also publishes the boundary condition that matters most for a small firm. If the knowledge base is under roughly 200,000 tokens — about 500 pages — the recommendation is to skip retrieval entirely and put the whole corpus in the prompt. Most advisory firms' actual standards library, meeting templates and process documentation fit inside 500 pages. A large share of the advisory RAG projects being sold in 2026 are solving a problem the buyer does not have.

06 · Measurement12 min

Measured Hallucination Rates Under Grounding

Retrieval failure is the first of two error sources. The second is the model reading a correctly retrieved passage and still writing something the passage does not support. Both are measured, by different people, on different benchmarks, and the numbers do not agree with the marketing.

Vectara's hallucination leaderboard supplies the cleanest measurement of the reading step. The model is handed the source document and given a prompt that instructs it to summarise using only the information in the passage, not to infer, and not to use internal knowledge. Every advantage is granted. In November 2025 Vectara replaced its dataset with a harder one: 7,700+ articles, documents up to 32,000 tokens, spanning law, medicine, finance, education and technology, split roughly evenly between low and high complexity. Rates rose across the board. Gemini-2.5-flash-lite led at 3.3%. Gemini-3-Pro came in at 13.6% and did not make the top 25. Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, GPT-OSS-120B, Grok-4 and DeepSeek-R1 all exceeded 10%.

Vectara reports two structural findings alongside the rankings: hallucination rates on high-complexity articles are consistently higher than on low-complexity articles, and rates rise with article length. A client meeting transcript and a 40-page plan document are the long, high-complexity end of that distribution, not the short news article the earlier benchmark used.

The professional-tools measurement is Stanford's. Magesh and colleagues ran the first preregistered evaluation of commercial legal research tools built on retrieval, against vendor claims of "hallucination-free" citations. Lexis+ AI hallucinated on more than 17% of queries, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research on roughly 33%, against 43% for GPT-4 with no retrieval. Grounding cut the rate by half to three quarters. It did not eliminate it, and the vendors' published claims were, in the authors' assessment, overstated.

07 · The build decision10 min

Grounding Compared With Fine-Tuning

Grounding changes what the model reads. Fine-tuning changes what the model is. Advisors are routinely sold the second when they need the first, and the distinguishing question is not accuracy but the cost of being wrong tomorrow.

Wolfram makes the underlying point without using either term. He observes that it is sufficient to tell the model something once, as part of the prompt, and it will use it — and that this is a clue about what the model is doing, because the alternative would be running a training algorithm and adjusting weights. He also notes the limit: the model can integrate what you tell it only if it rides in a fairly simple way on top of the framework it already has.

That limit is the honest case for fine-tuning and it is narrower than the sales deck. If your firm's house style, tone, and output format are unlike anything in the training corpus, tuning can help. If your firm's facts change — a contribution limit, a custodian's process, a model portfolio — tuning is the wrong instrument, because facts baked into weights cannot be corrected by editing a document, and the model cannot cite them.

08 · Process12 min

The Advisor Meeting Workflow and Its Failure Propagation

Prep, capture, summarise, follow-up, log. Five stages, and the reason to draw them as a chain rather than a list is that an error introduced at any stage is inherited, silently, by every stage after it — and the log is the one a regulator reads.

The chain has a property worth stating explicitly to a compliance officer: the only stage at which a human reliably reads the full output is the follow-up email, because the client will read it too. The CRM log is read by nobody until it matters. That is precisely inverted from where the risk sits.

09 · Architecture12 min

Note-Taker Architecture and the Extraction Step

Transcription, extraction, categorisation, follow-up. Transcription is a solved-enough engineering problem. Extraction is where a language model decides which sentences from ninety minutes of conversation constitute the record, and it is the step that carries essentially all of the professional risk.

Kitces' research frames the economics precisely: the average advisor spends more than one hour on meeting preparation and follow-up client-servicing tasks for every hour spent in client meetings, which means the prep-and-follow-up load, not the meeting load, is what caps client capacity. That is the number the category is being sold against.

The counterintuitive finding in the same research is the one this room should sit with. Adoption is highest among pure solo advisors with nobody to delegate to; it is about a third lower on two-to-three-person service teams; it rises again on larger teams, where the use case shifts from capturing notes to keeping absent colleagues informed; and it drops off precipitously at the ultra-high-net-worth end, where revenue per client supports having more humans in the room. Advisors producing the most extensive comprehensive plans use these tools at nearly four times the rate of those producing the narrowest targeted plans.

11 · Obligation12 min

Recording Consent and the Confidentiality Obligation

A note-taker is a recording device with a vendor attached. Two separate obligations attach to it: the state law that governs whether the recording may be made at all, and the professional duty of confidentiality that governs what may be disclosed to the vendor once it is.

On the first: federal law under the Wiretap Act sets a one-party consent floor, and states may impose more. Ten states appear on every published all-party list — California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Connecticut, Nevada, Oregon, Michigan and Vermont appear on some lists and not others, because the statutes are hybrid, the case law reads in exceptions, or there is no statute. Published counts in 2026 therefore range from eleven to twelve to fifteen depending on how the compiler treats the hybrids.

That disagreement is the teaching point, not a footnote. Ask a general-purpose model how many all-party consent states there are and you will get a single confident number, because the corpus contains many confident sources that disagree. Ask a retrieval-grounded tool and you will get a single confident number with a citation to whichever compiler its ranker surfaced. The citation makes the answer look verified. It has not been verified; it has been sourced.

Where recording crosses state lines, the practical rule courts have generally applied is the stricter state's law. California's civil remedy is the one that sets the exposure: Penal Code § 637.2 lets an injured person recover the greater of $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages, and § 637.2(c) states that suffering actual damages is not a prerequisite to the action. A recorded meeting series with one California participant is a per-recording exposure, not a per-lawsuit exposure.

On the second obligation: the practical test for a documentation vendor is not whether the contract mentions confidentiality. It is what the vendor does with the content — retention period, training use, subprocessors, where the transcript lives, and who at the vendor can read it. Iskowitz's treatment of note-taker compliance is the assigned reading on this; the questions worth putting to a vendor in writing are the ones whose answers you would have to disclose to a client.

12 · The record12 min

Documenting the Basis for a Recommendation

A record of what was said is not a record of why you recommended what you recommended. Extraction produces the first. The second has to be produced deliberately, and it is the part a note-taker structurally cannot generate, because the alternatives you rejected were never spoken aloud.

A defensible basis record contains four things and a note-taker can supply, at most, one and a half of them: the client circumstance that motivated the recommendation; the alternatives considered; the reason the recommendation was selected over those alternatives; and the client's response. The transcript contains the circumstance and the response. The alternatives and the reason existed in your head between meetings.

The workable pattern is to make the model interrogate you for the missing half rather than write it for you — the interview rewrite from Session 2, pointed at your own reasoning instead of at the client's facts. The prompt below does that. It is written to refuse to draft until it has the alternatives, which is the only property that matters.

Do this now — 5 minutes

Take the prompt, run it on a real recommendation

Copy this into your assistant and run it against a recommendation you made this month, using synthetic or de-identified facts. Count how many questions it asks before it drafts anything.

ROLE
You are a compliance reviewer at an SEC-registered investment adviser.
You do not draft the record. You interrogate me until the record is complete.

TASK
Build the basis-for-recommendation entry for one recommendation I made.

PROCEDURE
Ask me one question at a time. Do not draft anything until you have all four
elements below. If I answer vaguely, ask again with a narrower question.

1. CIRCUMSTANCE - the specific client fact that made this recommendation
   apply to this household and not to a generic household.
2. ALTERNATIVES - the other courses of action I actually considered.
   If I name fewer than two, ask what I ruled out and why it was ruled out.
3. SELECTION REASON - what made the recommendation preferable to each
   alternative named, stated as a comparison and not as a virtue.
4. CLIENT RESPONSE - what the client said when it was presented,
   including any disagreement or deferral.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not invent any fact. If I have not told you something, ask for it.
- Do not add a rationale I did not give you.
- Flag any element I supplied that is an assertion rather than an observation.

OUTPUT
When all four are complete: one paragraph per element, under 90 words each,
then a list of every element where my answer was thin.
