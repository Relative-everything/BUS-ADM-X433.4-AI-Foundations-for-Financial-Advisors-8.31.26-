Next-Token Probabilities for a Single Prompt

Wolfram's description of the mechanism is one sentence long: the model asks, over and over, “given the text so far, what should the next word be?” and each time adds a word. There is no separate step where it checks whether the sentence is true.

Take his example prompt. At each position the model produces a ranked list of candidate continuations with probabilities attached. Click a bar to select that continuation and watch the distribution recompute for the next position. Try running it twice: once always taking the top bar, once taking the second or third.

Prompt
Probability of the nth-ranked candidate token, first position
Wolfram reports that the fall-off across ranked candidates follows an n⁻¹ power law — the straight line on a log-log plot that he notes is characteristic of the general statistics of language. Rank 1 holds a large share; by rank 20 the candidates are individually negligible but collectively substantial.

What this rules out

A planner reading an AI answer to a tax question is not reading a lookup result. They are reading a sequence in which each word was chosen because it was a probable continuation of the words before it. A correct citation and a fabricated citation are both probable continuations. Nothing in the mechanism distinguishes them.

Appendix B2 · Foundation15 min

Laplace on Probability as a Measure of Ignorance

In 1814 Laplace proposed an intellect that knew every force and every position in nature. For such an intellect, he wrote, nothing would be uncertain; the future, like the past, would be present to its eyes. Probability, on his account, is not a property of the world. It is a property of the observer's incomplete information.

That distinction is the whole session. A client's 2025 return either does or does not permit a particular treatment. There is a fact. When the model gives you a probability distribution over answers, it is not reporting uncertainty in the tax code. It is reporting uncertainty about which continuation a human writer would have produced. Those are different objects, and the second one is not evidence about the first.

Two objects, side by side

Three cups

This demo always places the coin under the cup you do not lift, so everyone reaches the same beat. The chances shown are the correct ones for what you knew at each click.

The coin never moved. Your number moved three times, and each time it moved because what you knew changed. An AI chatbot works the same way, one layer down: it writes each word by guessing what comes next H, based on what it has read and what you typed. When it sounds certain, that certainty is your number, not the coin. It describes what the model has seen; it does not describe what is under the cup, in the tax code, or on your client’s return. Only lifting a cup changes the number. Only opening the source changes the answer.

The guessing sentence: Wolfram, S. (2023), “It’s Just Adding One Word at a Time”, the mechanism as reported, not quoted H. The cups are a constructed demonstration, labelled above.

Confidence in the output is not evidence about the world. It is evidence about the training data.

The practical consequence

Laplace's framing gives you the verification rule for the rest of the course. If probability is a statement about what the observer does not know, then reducing your own ignorance — opening the Code section, pulling the actual return, checking the effective date — is the only operation that changes the answer's reliability. Asking the model again, asking it more confidently, or asking it to double-check itself does not. Those operations resample the same distribution.

What the evidence says about Persona

Zheng and colleagues tested 162 distinct roles across four model families on 2,410 factual questions. Adding a persona to the system prompt did not improve accuracy against a no-persona control. The best-performing role varied question by question, and automated strategies for choosing a good role performed no better than picking one at random.

Effect of adding a persona to the system prompt on factual accuracy
Persona changes register, vocabulary and audience calibration. On objective questions with a checkable answer, it does not reliably change whether the answer is right.
Source: Zheng, M., Pei, J., Logeswaran, L., et al. (2024). When “A Helpful Assistant” Is Not Really Helpful: Personas in System Prompts Do Not Improve Performances of Large Language Models. Findings of EMNLP 2024. Study design and null result as reported H.

Where the effort should go instead

ComponentWhat it controlsEffect on whether the answer is correct, this course's ranking

Persona still earns its place: it sets the reading level for a client email and the terseness of a case note. It is not the lever for correctness, and a template that is only a persona is an unspecified template.

A related point on prompt length

Longer prompts are not better prompts. Specific prompts are better prompts, and specificity is usually shorter than the padding it replaces.

B4 · Temperature and Output Variance5 min · standard · hidden at the current appendix depth
Appendix B4 · Mechanism5 min

Temperature and Output Variance

Commit before the content runs

You send an identical prompt to the same model twice, an hour apart, with no other change. Which is true?

What can differ between two runs of the same prompt, an hour apart
  • The sampler. Above temperature 0 the model draws its next word from a spread of candidates, so two runs differ by design, and the vendor says even a setting of 0 is not fully deterministic.
  • Memory. What the account has remembered about you can change between the runs, and it is read on every turn.
  • Standing instructions. A profile or project instruction edited in the meantime travels with every prompt.
  • The rest of the window. Earlier turns, an attached file, a tool result, or a web search that returned different pages.
  • The version. On the API a model ID is a pinned snapshot and does not change under you; a chat product does not show you which build answered.
  • The machine. Batching and hardware effects the vendor does not expose can decide which of two near-equal words wins.
  • The clock. The system prompt usually carries the date, and an hour can cross midnight.
Sources: Anthropic, Messages API reference, temperature, retrieved 13 September 2026 H; Context windows H; Models overview, pinned snapshots, retrieved 13 September 2026 H; Claude help centre on personalisation and memory H H. The machine and the clock are stated as mechanisms, not measured.

Temperature is the dial that decides how often the model picks a lower-ranked next word instead of the top one. At 0 it takes the top word every time and the text goes flat and repetitive. Around 0.8 it reads naturally; Wolfram notes there is no theory behind that number: it is simply what works for essays. H

Move the slider, then run the same prompt five times and watch the spread.

0.0 — greedy: top bar every time; real APIs still vary0.8 — Wolfram's essay setting1.5 — degraded

Three consequences for an advisory practice

Consequence 01

A prompt is not an audit trail

The same prompt gives different text on every run, so a file that stores only the prompt cannot show what you relied on. In an annual compliance review that gets flagged. Keep the output you used, with the date and the model.

Consequence 02

Re-asking is not verification

Asking again draws a second sample from the same distribution. Two matching answers tell you about the model, not about the tax code.

Consequence 03

Control variance with the prompt

You cannot set temperature in the chat product, and current Claude models no longer accept it in the API either H. Constrain the output space instead: name the form, the length and the allowed values, and the spread collapses.

Appendix B5 · Failure mode16 min

Sampling Failure on a Task That Requires Counting

Wolfram trained a small transformer on a language with exactly one rule: parentheses must balance. The rule is simpler than any tax provision. The network still fails, and it fails in a specific and instructive way.

After roughly 400,000 trained weights, he reports, the network assigns about a 15 percent probability to a closing parenthesis in a position where closing would necessarily leave the sequence unbalanced. It is not confused about the rule in general. It is unable to hold a running count, because a feed-forward pass has no loop in which to keep one. He notes that even the full production model has a hard time matching parentheses in long sequences.

Sequence extension — click a token to append it

Illustrative weights, hand-set to show the failure; they are not a distribution and do not sum to one

Open count is tracked below the predictor, not by it.
Two kinds of task, one architecture
Tasks a human solves at a glance are the ones the network handles. Tasks requiring an explicit procedure carried across steps are the ones it approximates and then drops.
Source: Wolfram, S. (2023), “What Really Lets ChatGPT Work?” and the parenthesis-language section. Characterisation as reported H.

The mapping to your work

Confirming that a cited authority exists is a lookup against an index. Confirming that it says what the sentence claims is a comparison against a text. Confirming that it still says that is a check against an effective date. None of the three is a continuation task, and the architecture that writes the sentence is not the architecture that performs any of them. This is why grounding, retrieval, and your own eyes on the source are separate additions and not properties the model has.

The model is not bad at counting because it is undertrained. It is bad at counting because counting is not what it does.

Zero-Shot, One-Shot, Few-Shot

A “shot” is a worked example inside the prompt. The count is of examples. It is not a count of how much instruction you wrote, or of how many times you ran it.

Zero-shot gives the model an instruction and no example. One-shot gives it the instruction and exactly one example. Few-shot gives it several M. Label each prompt below before its feedback opens. A wrong label is the design intent.

A bare prompt is a fair first try. If the shape or the register comes out wrong, an example shows the model what you mean, and you then check that its content did not leak into the new draft. If a fact comes out wrong, an example will not fix it: a named source will (§02, Context).

The Seven-Step Process and the Delegation Line

The CFP Board's seven-step financial planning process is the structure your duty of care attaches to. Not every step is the same kind of work, and the steps that look most similar on paper have the least in common when you ask what a sampler could contribute.

Sort each step. Click a step, then click a column. Change your mind as often as you like. The key appears when all seven are placed, and you are expected to argue with it.

The delegation decision

Steps 2 and 4 stay with you. Step 2 is eliciting what a client actually wants, which is often not what they first say. Step 4 is the judgment your registration, your credential and your liability attach to. A model can draft language for either; it cannot perform either. Steps 3 and 7 are the opposite case: high-volume, structured and checkable against a source, which is where the measurable time recovery lives.

The profession has already drawn this line. 52.2% of advisors use AI search and generative tools M, while roughly 3% report relying on a model’s recommendation, a directional figure M. Where steps 1, 5 and 6 sit is your firm’s choice.

B8 · Cost Per Task Versus Cost Per Token6 min · standard · hidden at the current appendix depth
Appendix B8 · Economics6 min

Cost Per Task Versus Cost Per Token

Dollars per million tokens measures the wrong thing. Two models two dollars apart per million tokens can differ fourfold in what a finished piece of work costs, because they burn different numbers of tokens getting there.

Artificial Analysis publishes an Intelligence Index score and the dollar cost of running that benchmark through each model. Plotted together, they scramble the price-sheet ranking.

What a task is

A task is one finished unit of work you would otherwise do yourself, priced by every token the model reads and writes to finish it at a quality you accept, retries and reasoning included. Five from an advisory week:

Task

A meeting summary from your notes

Your rough notes in, a client-ready summary out. One task.

Task

A 40-page trust into one plain-English page

Long input, short output. One task, dearer on the input side.

Task

A week of inbound email sorted by urgency

Fifty messages, one pass. One task, or fifty small ones; price it the way you run it.

Task

An RMD reminder letter for one household

Short input, short output, repeated across the book. The cheapest kind.

Task

Beneficiary designations pulled from a statement PDF

Extraction against a document. Judgment-light, checkable line by line.

What a benchmark task is

The testers behind the chart below hold a fixed set of questions. Every model gets the same questions, at its highest reasoning setting, and every answer is kept and scored. Cost, not time, is what the chart plots: every token the model read and wrote across the run, added up and priced at that model’s published rates. One pass through the whole set is the task M. The Interview Rewrite (Reverse Prompting)

Wolfram makes an observation that most prompting advice skips: you can tell the model something once, as part of the prompt, and it will use it. The prompt is not a request. It is the entire evidentiary record available to the model at the moment it answers.

You would not build a recommendation from a client meeting in which you asked one question. The technique below inverts the order: instead of writing a better prompt, you make the model conduct the intake.

Four failure types, in ascending order of danger

The Stanford taxonomy separates fabrication from misgrounding. This question adds a third and a fourth that matter in retirement planning: an age the Code has since amended, so that an older answer fits an earlier birth cohort, and guidance that is cited as if it were law.

Type 01

Fabrication

The authority does not exist. Fails an existence search in fifteen seconds. The least dangerous failure, because it is the only one that is trivially catchable.

Type 02

Misgrounding

Real, retrievable, correctly formatted, and does not support the proposition attached to it. Requires reading the authority.

Type 03

Superseded

Correct when issued, changed since. Training data has a cutoff; the Code does not.

Type 04

Cited as law, but only guidance

A real publication or form instruction, described correctly and presented as if it were the statute. The citation is genuine and the figure is right. What is wrong is the weight. Neither an existence check nor a text check catches this one.

Citation triage — classify each

Six items an AI assistant returned when asked what authority supports the age at which Meg and David must start required minimum distributions. Classify each. All six are exercise material. Do not carry any classification into practice without checking the authority yourself, which is the entire point.

The verification order

Four checks, and they must run in this sequence, because each one is only meaningful if the previous one passed.

Check 01 — Existence

Does the authority exist

Section number, revenue procedure number, case reporter and page. A search against the Code or the reporter settles it. Fifteen seconds.

Check 02 — Text

Does it say what the sentence claims

Open it and read the operative language. This is the check that catches misgrounding and the one most often skipped.

Check 03 — Currency

Is it still operative

Amended, superseded, modified by a later revenue procedure, or subject to a sunset. How to check: for a ruling, later Bulletins list it as modified, superseded or obsoleted; for a case, look for an appeal, a reversal or a later statute. Tax law moves by statute, ruling and court decision, so no source is a permanent record.

Check 04 — Applicability

Does it reach these facts

The provision is real, correctly stated and current, and governs a different fact pattern from your client's. Only you can perform this one.

Existence is the floor of verification and never the ceiling.

Live run, 2 minutes

Ask your AI tool: What authority sets the age at which a person born in 1968 must begin required minimum distributions from a 403(b), and what are the principal risks of relying on the answer? Run all four checks on the first authority it returns, plus a fifth: for a publication or form instruction, confirm whether it is the law or only guidance about the law. Time yourself.

That elapsed time is the real cost of the workflow. It belongs in your final project's time comparison, and leaving it out is the most common way these projects overstate their savings.

Appendix B9 · Diagnostic5 min

Prompt Specification Triage

Three categories, not two. A prompt can be underspecified. It can be fully specified and still produce output nobody can check. Or it can be specified in a way that makes checking possible. Only the third is usable in a fiduciary practice.

Category A

Underspecified

The output shape, the source boundary, or the operation itself is left to the model to choose.

Category B

Specified, unverifiable

You know exactly what will come back. You have no procedure for confirming it is right.

Category C

Specified and verifiable

The output is constrained to a form where a named source settles whether each element is correct.

Several of these are designed to be mis-sorted. Read the whole prompt before choosing.

06 · Discussion15 min

Specification Cost Against Task Cost

The question, stated once and not softened: if a reliable prompt has to be specified as carefully as §02 required, has the tool saved time, or has it moved the work from doing the task to specifying the task?

This is not a rhetorical question with a preferred answer. Both positions are defensible and the room will divide. Commit before anyone speaks.

Predicted split: close to even on the first vote. The “saved” side leans on reuse across clients; the “moved” side leans on the check that cannot be reused. Expect the second vote to move only if someone names a fact.

Phase one: position, 2 minutes

Vote. Your vote locks and cannot be changed. Nobody sees the distribution yet, including you.

Phase two: defence, 7 minutes

Two speakers per side, alternating, ninety seconds each. The minority side speaks first — they expected to have to defend and are the more prepared for it. Nobody rebuts during this phase.

Speaking timer
1:30 Turn 0 of 4

Phase three: complication, 4 minutes

One fact neither side has raised. Nobody who has already spoken speaks again.

Complication

The verification time is not optional and it is not amortised

§05 timed it on one question. Every run of the template on a new client produces new citations, and each one needs the same four checks. In this session’s Roth email the wrong citation was a real section with the wrong age, and only the clients’ birth years exposed it. A template carries the specification forward. It cannot carry the birth years.

Drafting saved against checking added
