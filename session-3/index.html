01 · Mechanism5 min

Embeddings and the Coordinates of Meaning Space

Wolfram's definition is the one to hold: an embedding is a way to represent the essence of something by an array of numbers, with the property that nearby things are represented by nearby numbers. A word embedding lays words out in a meaning space in which words nearby in meaning appear nearby in the space.

Two details matter more than the definition. First, how the arrays are produced: Wolfram measures how similar the environments are in which different words appear, across five billion words of web text. Nothing in that procedure inspects meaning. It inspects company. Second, the arrays are long — 768 numbers per token for GPT-2, 12,288 for GPT-3. Every two-dimensional picture of meaning space is a projection from hundreds or thousands of axes, and projection destroys information.

The map is hand-built — positions assigned, not learned — but the arithmetic is real. Clicking a term computes Euclidean distance to every other term and ranks them: the same operation a retriever performs, at two dimensions instead of a thousand. Vocabulary from the Cole file.

02 · The failure this creates5 min

Distributional Similarity and Referential Difference

Wolfram's own worked example contains the failure mode. He explains that "alligator" and "crocodile" will often appear almost interchangeably in otherwise similar sentences, and that this is why they are placed near each other, while "turnip" and "eagle" do not appear in similar sentences and are placed far apart.

The property being measured is interchangeability of context. Alligators and crocodiles are different animals; the embedding does not encode that they differ, only that people write about them the same way. In the Cole file that is the central hazard, because the governing documents are written in near-identical language and differ in one condition each.

Distributional proximity is the signal. Referential identity is what you needed.

03 · RAG, mechanically6 min

Retrieval Mechanics: Chunking, Indexing, and Ranking

Retrieval-augmented generation is four operations, none of them mysterious. Split the source documents into chunks. Convert each chunk into a vector. At query time, convert the query into a vector by the same procedure and rank the chunks by similarity. Paste the top-ranked chunks into the prompt and instruct the model to answer only from them.

The advisory use case is in front of you: governing terms spread across a 2011 will, a 2011 trust, a 2014 buy-sell, a 2023 appraisal and two unexecuted drafts, none read end to end this year. The model is not asked to know the Coles. It is asked to read the three paragraphs the ranker handed it. Everything that goes wrong went wrong in the ranking or in the reading.

The retriever is real. It builds an IDF index over ten chunks of the Cole set, weights each term by tf × ln(1 + N/df), and ranks by cosine similarity. Two of four preset queries are engineered to fail — not a bug, the alligator-and-crocodile property arriving in the valuation question the whole transfer turns on.

Chunk Size and Condition Orphaning

Chunk size is the only parameter in a retrieval system a non-engineer will ever be asked to choose, and it decides whether a rule arrives with the condition that governs it.

Anthropic's engineering write-up states the problem in one example: a chunk reads "the company's revenue grew by 3% over the previous quarter," and nothing in that chunk says which company or which quarter. The subject was two paragraphs earlier, in a different chunk. The embedding of the orphaned chunk carries none of it.

The Cole version is worse, because the orphaned element is not the subject but the trigger. Article VII of the 2014 buy-sell states a valuation formula, and it states it in a sentence that never names the event that invokes it. Severed from the sentence before it, the formula reads as CPC's valuation method. It is not. It is the valuation method for a death, a disability or a withdrawal — none of which is happening here. Drag the slider and watch the trigger separate from the formula.

Anthropic measured the retrieval step alone, before any generation, as the share of queries for which the correct chunk was absent from the top twenty returned. The baseline was 5.7%. The best configuration reached 1.9%.

Two things from that pair. The size: contextual chunks, a lexical index alongside the vector index, and a reranking pass cut failures by two thirds. The floor: 1.9% is not zero — and this is retrieval measured in isolation, on a benchmark built by the vendor of the technique.

Those rates describe how often the right chunk is missing. They say nothing about the case where a plausible wrong one is present instead — which is what the Cole file produces, and what you are about to predict.

05 · Measurement5 min

Measured Hallucination Rates Under Grounding

The spiral, stated once: Session 1 was why hallucination happens, tonight is how often it happens under the strongest available remedy, and Session 4 is what it costs you when it happens anyway.

Retrieval failure is the first error source. The second is the model reading a correctly retrieved passage and still writing something it does not support. Both are measured, and neither agrees with the marketing.

Vectara's leaderboard is the cleanest measurement of the reading step. The model gets the source document and a prompt telling it to summarise using only that passage, not to infer, not to use internal knowledge. Every advantage granted. In November 2025 Vectara moved to a harder dataset: 7,700+ articles up to 32,000 tokens across law, medicine, finance, education and technology, split evenly between low and high complexity. Rates rose across the board. Gemini-2.5-flash-lite led at 3.3%; Gemini-3-Pro came in at 13.6% and missed the top 25; Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, GPT-OSS-120B, Grok-4 and DeepSeek-R1 all exceeded 10%.

Two structural findings alongside the rankings: rates are consistently higher on high-complexity articles, and rise with length. A 2014 buy-sell and a 2023 appraisal sit at the long, high-complexity end of that distribution — not the short news article the earlier benchmark used.

Stanford supplies the professional-tools measurement. Magesh and colleagues ran the first preregistered evaluation of retrieval-built legal research tools against vendor claims of "hallucination-free" citations. Lexis+ AI hallucinated on more than 17% of queries, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research on roughly 33%, against 43% for GPT-4 ungrounded. Grounding cut the rate by half to three quarters, did not eliminate it, and the vendors' claims were — in the authors' assessment — overstated.

Appendix C2 · Comparison14 min

Grounding Compared With Fine-Tuning

Grounding changes what the model reads. Fine-tuning changes what the model is. Advisors are routinely sold the second when they need the first, and the distinguishing question is not accuracy but the cost of being wrong tomorrow.

Wolfram makes the underlying point without using either term. He observes that it is sufficient to tell the model something once, as part of the prompt, and it will use it — and that this is a clue about what the model is doing, because the alternative would be running a training algorithm and adjusting weights. He also notes the limit: the model can integrate what you tell it only if it rides in a fairly simple way on top of the framework it already has.

That limit is the honest case for fine-tuning and it is narrower than the sales deck. If your house style and output format are unlike anything in the training corpus, tuning can help. If your facts change — and the Cole file is nothing but facts that change, a discount that needs re-verifying and a buy-sell that needs amending — tuning is the wrong instrument, because facts baked into weights cannot be corrected by editing a document and cannot be cited.

07 · Architecture5 min

Note-Taker Architecture and the Extraction Step

Transcription, extraction, categorisation, follow-up. Transcription is a solved-enough engineering problem. Extraction is where a language model decides which sentences from ninety minutes of conversation constitute the record, and it carries essentially all of the professional risk.

Kitces frames the economics: the average advisor spends more than an hour on prep and follow-up for every hour in client meetings. The prep-and-follow-up load, not the meeting load, caps client capacity. That is the number the category is sold against.

The counterintuitive finding is worth sitting with. Adoption is highest among solo advisors with nobody to delegate to, about a third lower on two-to-three-person teams, higher again on larger teams where the use shifts to keeping absent colleagues informed, and drops off sharply at the ultra-high-net-worth end where revenue per client supports more humans in the room. Advisors producing the most comprehensive plans use these tools at nearly four times the rate of those producing the narrowest. Meg Cole sits in the segment with the lowest adoption and the highest stakes.

Recording Consent and the Confidentiality Obligation

Requires instructor verification before it is taught as settled This session characterises recording law and professional-conduct obligations. None of it is legal advice, and the state counts genuinely disagree across published compilations. Verify before teaching any of it as settled:
  • 18 U.S.C. § 2511 as a federal one-party-consent floor, and whether your state imposes more.
  • The all-party-consent state count. Published 2026 compilations range from eleven to fifteen depending on how hybrid statutes are treated. The disagreement is the teaching point; do not resolve it to a single number at the podium.
  • Cal. Penal Code § 637.2(a)(1) and (c) — the greater of $5,000 per violation or treble actual damages, with actual damages not a prerequisite.
  • The stricter-state rule for recordings crossing state lines. Courts have generally applied it; confirm the position in your jurisdiction.
  • The duty of confidentiality as it applies to disclosing client content to a documentation vendor, under your own firm's policy and your credentialing body's code.

A note-taker is a recording device with a vendor attached. Two separate obligations attach: the state law governing whether the recording may be made at all, and the professional duty of confidentiality governing what may be disclosed to the vendor once it is.

On the first: federal law under the Wiretap Act sets a one-party consent floor, and states may impose more. Ten states appear on every published all-party list [UNCONFIRMED] — California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Connecticut, Nevada, Oregon, Michigan and Vermont appear on some lists and not others, because the statutes are hybrid, the case law reads in exceptions, or there is no statute. Published counts in 2026 range from eleven to twelve to fifteen depending on how the compiler treats the hybrids.

That disagreement is the teaching point, not a footnote. Ask a general-purpose model how many all-party consent states there are and you get a single confident number, because the corpus contains many confident sources that disagree. Ask a retrieval-grounded tool and you get a single confident number with a citation to whichever compiler its ranker surfaced. The citation makes the answer look verified. It has not been verified; it has been sourced. That is the same failure you watched happen to Article VII an hour ago.

Where recording crosses state lines, the practical rule courts have generally applied is the stricter state's law. California's civil remedy sets the exposure: Penal Code § 637.2 lets an injured person recover the greater of $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages, and § 637.2(c) states that suffering actual damages is not a prerequisite. A recorded meeting series with one California participant is a per-recording exposure, not a per-lawsuit exposure.

On the second obligation: the practical test for a documentation vendor is not whether the contract mentions confidentiality. It is what the vendor does with the content — retention period, training use, subprocessors, where the transcript lives, and who at the vendor can read it. The Cole file carries exactly three confidentiality landmines: that a competitor approached the company and that Meg holds a memorandum assessing him, neither of which Nathan knows; the unexecuted transfer plan and the discount being claimed, which exist only in a privileged attorney memorandum; and the endowment Meg intends for the charity whose board she chairs, which that board has not been told about.

09 · Documentation6 min

Documenting the Basis for a Recommendation

A record of what was said is not a record of why you recommended what you recommended. Extraction produces the first. The second must be produced deliberately, and a note-taker structurally cannot generate it, because the alternatives you rejected were never spoken aloud.

Take the live decision in the Cole file. The block of non-voting units at closing is being sold to the trust on a demand note rather than gifted to it. Nobody in the meeting will say why. The reasoning happened between meetings, in the attorney's head and yours, and if it is not written down at the time it is not recoverable later — not because memory fails, but because a reconstruction written after an examination begins is worth approximately nothing.

The second half of the exercise is harder and more valuable. If the return supporting this transaction were examined, the discount would have to be defended on the evidence that existed when the transaction closed. The case supplies a 2023 appraisal that concluded a value for the whole company and contains no discount study at all, and no appraiser has been engaged since. There is nothing behind the discount that could be checked, and a competitor has now made an approach nobody has answered. Sort what would help from what would not.
