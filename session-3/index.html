01 · Mechanism8 min

Embeddings and the Coordinates of Meaning Space

Wolfram's definition is the one to hold: an embedding is a way to represent the essence of something by an array of numbers, with the property that nearby things are represented by nearby numbers. A word embedding lays words out in a meaning space in which words nearby in meaning appear nearby in the space.

Two details from the reading matter more than the definition. The first is how the arrays are produced. Wolfram describes looking at large amounts of text — five billion words from the web in his example — and measuring how similar the environments are in which different words appear. Nothing in that procedure inspects meaning. It inspects company. The second is that the arrays are long: Wolfram gives 768 numbers per token for GPT-2 and 12,288 for GPT-3. Every two-dimensional picture of meaning space, his and the one below, is a projection of something with hundreds or thousands of axes down to two, and projection destroys information.

The map below is an illustrative hand-built layout — the positions were assigned, not learned. The arithmetic run on it is real: clicking a term computes Euclidean distance to every other term in the displayed coordinates and ranks them. That is the same operation a retriever performs, at two dimensions instead of a thousand. The vocabulary is the Cole file's.

02 · The failure this creates7 min

Distributional Similarity and Referential Difference

Wolfram's own worked example contains the failure mode. He explains that "alligator" and "crocodile" will often appear almost interchangeably in otherwise similar sentences, and that this is why they are placed near each other, while "turnip" and "eagle" do not appear in similar sentences and are placed far apart.

Read that as an engineer rather than as a reader. The property being measured is interchangeability of context. Alligators and crocodiles are different animals. The embedding does not encode that they are different; it encodes that people write about them the same way. In the Cole file this is not an approximation problem, it is the central hazard, because the documents that govern this household are written in near-identical language and differ in one condition each.

Distributional proximity is the signal. Referential identity is what you needed.

03 · RAG, mechanically12 min

Retrieval Mechanics: Chunking, Indexing, and Ranking

Retrieval-augmented generation is four operations, none of them mysterious. Split the source documents into chunks. Convert each chunk into a vector. At query time, convert the query into a vector by the same procedure and rank the chunks by similarity. Paste the top-ranked chunks into the prompt and instruct the model to answer only from them.

The advisory use case is the one in front of you: a household whose governing terms live across a 2011 will, a 2011 trust, a 2014 buy-sell, a 2023 appraisal and two unexecuted drafts, none of which any human has read end to end this year. The model is not being asked to know the Coles. It is being asked to read the three paragraphs the ranker handed it. Everything that can go wrong in the answer either went wrong in the ranking, or went wrong in the reading.

The retriever below is real, not a mock-up. It builds an inverse-document-frequency index over ten chunks of the Cole document set, weights each term by tf × ln(1 + N/df), and ranks by cosine similarity. Two of the four preset queries are engineered to fail. The failure is not a bug in the code; it is the alligator-and-crocodile property, arriving in an $18,000,000 valuation question.

04 · The engineering decision that breaks it10 min

Chunk Size and Condition Orphaning

Chunk size is the only parameter in a retrieval system a non-engineer will ever be asked to choose, and it decides whether a rule arrives with the condition that governs it.

Anthropic's engineering write-up states the problem in one example: a chunk reads "the company's revenue grew by 3% over the previous quarter," and nothing in that chunk says which company or which quarter. The subject was two paragraphs earlier, in a different chunk. The embedding of the orphaned chunk carries none of it.

The Cole version is worse, because the orphaned element is not the subject but the trigger. Article VII of the 2014 buy-sell states a valuation formula, and it states it in a sentence that never names the event that invokes it. Severed from the sentence before it, the formula reads as CPC's valuation method. It is not. It is the valuation method for a death, a disability or a withdrawal — none of which is happening here. Drag the slider and watch the trigger separate from the formula.

05 · Measurement9 min

Measured Retrieval Failure Rates

Anthropic measured the retrieval step alone, before any generation, as the share of queries for which the correct chunk was absent from the top twenty returned. The baseline was 5.7%. The best configuration reached 1.9%.

Two things are worth extracting from that pair. First, the direction and the size: adding context to each chunk before embedding, adding a lexical index alongside the vector index, and adding a reranking pass cut failures by two thirds. Second, the floor: 1.9% is not zero, and this is the retrieval step measured in isolation, on a benchmark built by the vendor of the technique.

Those rates describe how often the right chunk is missing. They say nothing about the case where the right chunk is missing and a plausible wrong one is present — which is what the Cole file produces, and what you are about to predict.

06 · Measurement10 min

Measured Hallucination Rates Under Grounding

The spiral, stated once: Session 1 was why hallucination happens, tonight is how often it happens under the strongest available remedy, and Session 4 is what it costs you when it happens anyway.

Retrieval failure is the first of two error sources. The second is the model reading a correctly retrieved passage and still writing something the passage does not support. Both are measured, by different people, on different benchmarks, and the numbers do not agree with the marketing.

Vectara's hallucination leaderboard supplies the cleanest measurement of the reading step. The model is handed the source document and given a prompt instructing it to summarise using only the information in the passage, not to infer, and not to use internal knowledge. Every advantage is granted. In November 2025 Vectara replaced its dataset with a harder one: 7,700+ articles, documents up to 32,000 tokens, spanning law, medicine, finance, education and technology, split roughly evenly between low and high complexity. Rates rose across the board. Gemini-2.5-flash-lite led at 3.3%. Gemini-3-Pro came in at 13.6% and did not make the top 25. Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, GPT-OSS-120B, Grok-4 and DeepSeek-R1 all exceeded 10%.

Vectara reports two structural findings alongside the rankings: rates on high-complexity articles are consistently higher than on low-complexity articles, and rates rise with article length. A 2014 buy-sell agreement and a 2023 appraisal report are the long, high-complexity end of that distribution, not the short news article the earlier benchmark used.

The professional-tools measurement is Stanford's. Magesh and colleagues ran the first preregistered evaluation of commercial legal research tools built on retrieval, against vendor claims of "hallucination-free" citations. Lexis+ AI hallucinated on more than 17% of queries, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research on roughly 33%, against 43% for GPT-4 with no retrieval. Grounding cut the rate by half to three quarters. It did not eliminate it, and the vendors' published claims were, in the authors' assessment, overstated.

07 · The build decision7 min

Grounding Compared With Fine-Tuning

Grounding changes what the model reads. Fine-tuning changes what the model is. Advisors are routinely sold the second when they need the first, and the distinguishing question is not accuracy but the cost of being wrong tomorrow.

Wolfram makes the underlying point without using either term. He observes that it is sufficient to tell the model something once, as part of the prompt, and it will use it — and that this is a clue about what the model is doing, because the alternative would be running a training algorithm and adjusting weights. He also notes the limit: the model can integrate what you tell it only if it rides in a fairly simple way on top of the framework it already has.

That limit is the honest case for fine-tuning and it is narrower than the sales deck. If your house style and output format are unlike anything in the training corpus, tuning can help. If your facts change — and the Cole file is nothing but facts that change, a discount that needs re-verifying and a buy-sell that needs amending — tuning is the wrong instrument, because facts baked into weights cannot be corrected by editing a document and cannot be cited.

09 · Architecture9 min

Note-Taker Architecture and the Extraction Step

Transcription, extraction, categorisation, follow-up. Transcription is a solved-enough engineering problem. Extraction is where a language model decides which sentences from ninety minutes of conversation constitute the record, and it carries essentially all of the professional risk.

Kitces' research frames the economics precisely: the average advisor spends more than one hour on meeting preparation and follow-up client-servicing tasks for every hour spent in client meetings, which means the prep-and-follow-up load, not the meeting load, caps client capacity. That is the number the category is sold against.

The counterintuitive finding in the same research is the one this room should sit with. Adoption is highest among pure solo advisors with nobody to delegate to; about a third lower on two-to-three-person service teams; higher again on larger teams, where the use case shifts to keeping absent colleagues informed; and it drops off precipitously at the ultra-high-net-worth end, where revenue per client supports more humans in the room. Advisors producing the most extensive comprehensive plans use these tools at nearly four times the rate of those producing the narrowest targeted plans. Meg Cole sits in the segment with the lowest adoption and the highest stakes.

11 · Obligation9 min

Recording Consent and the Confidentiality Obligation

A note-taker is a recording device with a vendor attached. Two separate obligations attach: the state law governing whether the recording may be made at all, and the professional duty of confidentiality governing what may be disclosed to the vendor once it is.

On the first: federal law under the Wiretap Act sets a one-party consent floor, and states may impose more. Ten states appear on every published all-party list — California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Connecticut, Nevada, Oregon, Michigan and Vermont appear on some lists and not others, because the statutes are hybrid, the case law reads in exceptions, or there is no statute. Published counts in 2026 range from eleven to twelve to fifteen depending on how the compiler treats the hybrids.

That disagreement is the teaching point, not a footnote. Ask a general-purpose model how many all-party consent states there are and you get a single confident number, because the corpus contains many confident sources that disagree. Ask a retrieval-grounded tool and you get a single confident number with a citation to whichever compiler its ranker surfaced. The citation makes the answer look verified. It has not been verified; it has been sourced. That is the same failure you watched happen to Article VII an hour ago.

Where recording crosses state lines, the practical rule courts have generally applied is the stricter state's law. California's civil remedy sets the exposure: Penal Code § 637.2 lets an injured person recover the greater of $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages, and § 637.2(c) states that suffering actual damages is not a prerequisite. A recorded meeting series with one California participant is a per-recording exposure, not a per-lawsuit exposure.

On the second obligation: the practical test for a documentation vendor is not whether the contract mentions confidentiality. It is what the vendor does with the content — retention period, training use, subprocessors, where the transcript lives, and who at the vendor can read it. The Cole file carries three facts that would end a client relationship if they surfaced: that Meg is exploring a sale, which Nathan does not know; the unexecuted IDGT plan and the discount being claimed; and the bare fact that Meg is a client of the firm at all.

12 · The record10 min

Documenting the Basis for a Recommendation

A record of what was said is not a record of why you recommended what you recommended. Extraction produces the first. The second must be produced deliberately, and a note-taker structurally cannot generate it, because the alternatives you rejected were never spoken aloud.

Take the live decision in the Cole file. The 40% non-voting interest is being sold to the trust for a $4,968,000 note rather than gifted to it. Nobody in the meeting will say why. The reasoning happened between meetings, in the attorney's head and yours, and if it is not written down at the time it is not recoverable later — not because memory fails, but because a reconstruction written after an examination begins is worth approximately nothing.

The second half of the exercise is harder and more valuable. If the return supporting this transaction were examined, the 31% discount would have to be defended on the evidence that existed when the transaction closed. The case supplies a 2023 appraisal that has never been re-verified and a buyer who has expressed informal interest. Sort what would help from what would not.
