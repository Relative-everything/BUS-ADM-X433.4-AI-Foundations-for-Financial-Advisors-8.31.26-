What the file records and no session resolves.

  • The stale designation. David's $210,000 rollover IRA still names his mother, Dorothy Cole, who died in 2021, with no contingent beneficiary (form dated 11 May 2009). By the custodian's default it would pay to his estate. It also blocks a clean backdoor Roth contribution for David (the aggregation rule); Meg, who holds no pre-tax IRA, is not blocked.
  • The 2011 documents. Two wills with no trust, a guardian clause for children of whom one is now an adult, and a successor agent who has died. The residence passes by joint tenancy; Illinois offers a transfer-on-death instrument the Coles have not used.
  • The 2024 memo and the 2026 plan. The prior adviser's Roth conversion memo (3 October 2024) says the 401(k) does not permit in-plan Roth conversion; the plan description amended 1 January 2026 says it does. Session 3's retrieval exercise grounds on both.
  • No long-term care coverage for either spouse, and Ruth's situation is the live example of the cost. David's group life ($370,000) ends at separation; Meg's $500,000 term policy ends in 2030.
  • The question that runs through every session. How much of the $1,720,000 of pre-tax money to convert to Roth, and in which years of the window, with the federal bracket in the year of conversion and the Medicare premium two years later as the two prices, and Illinois charging nothing. No session answers it; each works on a part of it.

The question that runs through every session

The Coles hold $1,720,000 in pre-tax accounts, 70.3% of what they have, and a window of years with no paycheck and no required distribution: Meg 2029 to 2042, David 2030 to 2041. How much do they convert to Roth, in which years, when the federal bracket is one price and the Medicare premium two years later is the other?

No session answers it. Every session works on a different part of it. The arithmetic is in the case: the 24% bracket has $162,680 of room in 2026 and a Medicare premium is set on income from two years before; Illinois taxes none of it. Nothing in the case says how much to convert, and neither does this course.

Requires instructor verification before it is taught as settled Every tax and legal characterisation attached to this case is posed as a question, not a holding. Verify before teaching any of it as settled:
  • Whether Illinois leaves the taxable amount of a traditional-to-Roth conversion untaxed, so that a conversion costs the Coles nothing at the state level. CASE.md §E.2 carries the retirement income subtraction (35 ILCS 5/203(a)(2)(F)) at [M], from a search excerpt of the primary domain and not the full page.
  • Whether the Medicare look-back works the way the case states it: a conversion in one year sets the income-related premium two years later, and Form SSA-44 can ask for a more recent year after a work stoppage. CASE.md §F.3 carries both at [M].
  • Whether the Coles’ estate crosses the Illinois estate tax line: a $4,000,000 exclusion that is not indexed and not portable, tested on the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts, carried at [M] (35 ILCS 405). CASE.md §H.4 puts the Coles at $3,935,800 with life insurance counted, $64,200 below it, and states no conclusion.
  • The four figures CASE.md itself labels [L], the weakest in the file: the HSA age-55 catch-up of $1,000 (no 2026 restatement found), the American opportunity credit phase-out (the 2026 figures rest on a draft form), the Illinois exemption allowance of $2,925 (from the 2026 withholding booklet), and the DuPage County effective property tax rate (no government rate table was readable). This is the live course site. Every lesson is updated here directly — there are no files to download and no version numbers to track. Whatever you see on this page is current.

Each lesson is built as a core session of about an hour that always runs, followed by an appendix of optional depth. Gold links in the core point down to the appendix material that belongs at that moment; every appendix section links back. A control at the top of each lesson filters the appendix by depth — foundational, standard or advanced — or hides it entirely for the one-hour version.

Each lesson runs entirely in your browser. Your progress and any notes you enter stay on your own device and are never uploaded.
