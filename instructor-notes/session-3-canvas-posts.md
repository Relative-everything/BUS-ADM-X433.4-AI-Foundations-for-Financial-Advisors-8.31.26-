## Post 2 · Corrections from Sessions 1 and 2 (post before Monday)

Subject: Corrections to things I said in Sessions 1 and 2

Part of this course is checking claims against a primary source. These are mine. Each carries the
confidence I have in the correction. Two of them I will also say aloud on Monday.

**From Session 2**

1. **Output tokens cost about five times input, not about twice.** Haiku 4.5 $1 in / $5 out,
   Sonnet 5 $2 / $10, Opus 5 $5 / $25, Fable 5.1 $10 / $50 per million tokens. The conclusion I
   drew, that cost per task is not cost per token, stands; the multiple was wrong. High confidence.
2. **A missed portability election is not always permanently lost.** For an estate with no filing
   requirement under §6018(a), Rev. Proc. 2022-32 allows a simplified election on or before the
   fifth anniversary of death, by filing a complete Form 706 marked "FILED PURSUANT TO REV. PROC.
   2022-32 TO ELECT PORTABILITY UNDER § 2010(c)(5)(A)". It superseded Rev. Proc. 2017-34's two-year
   window. "Permanently lost" is right only for estates that were required to file. High
   confidence, and this is the one that could matter to a client: the Session 2 prompt-critique
   item about a spouse who died in 2021 sits inside that window.

**From Session 1**

3. **The watermark does not identify your account.** I said Claude output could be traced to the
   account that generated it. The narrow correction is that a text watermark, where one exists,
   identifies the generator, not a person, organisation or chat. Whether current Claude output is
   watermarked at all is something I am confirming against Anthropic's own page and will say once
   I have read it. High confidence on the narrow correction.
4. **Cached input is re-billed at about ten percent, not "at least five, probably fifty".** High.
5. **Temperature zero is an accepted setting, not "impossible".** It is still not fully
   deterministic. High.
6. **The intra-family loan thresholds.** IRC §7872(c)(2) sets the de minimis exception at
   $10,000 for gift loans between individuals; $100,000 is the separate ceiling in §7872(d)(1)
   under which imputed interest is capped at the borrower's net investment income. I ran the two
   together. High.
7. **Input is not cheap "because they train on it".** Commercial and API data is not used for
   training. Per the 2026-09-07 claims audit.
8. **The squeeze, freeze and burn paper.** The author I named does not exist. The paper is Marvin
   E. Blum's, in the NAEPC Journal of Estate & Tax Planning, issue 7. Per the claims audit.
9. **Fable is exactly twice the per-token price of Opus, not "over twice" or "six times".** High.
10. **The input box is not the context window.** The window is everything sent with each message,
    including the whole conversation so far. High.
11. **Two leaderboards were conflated.** The Intelligence Index is Artificial Analysis's, not
    LiveBench's. Medium.
12. **Navajo has a written form**, standardised since the late 1930s. The point I was making
    stands: it is a low-resource language for these models because little of it is digitised.
    High.
13. **The Cole company is a C corporation.** I described the trust's income as S-corporation
    pass-through. It is not: CPC has never made an S election and the "burn" runs on dividends.
    High, from the case file.
14. **FP Alpha is an AI-driven platform, not a deterministic calculator.** Its own tax-return
    reading routes low-confidence extractions to human reviewers, which is the discernment point
    I was making, not a counter-example to it. High.
15. **The eCFR is at ecfr.gov, not .com**, and it carries Treasury regulations (26 CFR); the Code
    itself is 26 U.S.C. Two bodies of authority I ran together. High.
16. **The known-knowns 2x2 is not Anthropic's.** It predates them by decades. Medium on the
    attribution history, high that it is not Anthropic's.
17. **The Session 1 probability-against-rank curve was illustrative, not measured.** Per the
    claims audit.

If you spot anything else, say so. Being called out is the point.
