# Session 4 teaching aid, 2027 term

Plain-text copy of `session-4-teaching-aid.pdf`, for a phone. The PDF is the one to print: three landscape pages, page 1 the flow, page 2 the setup, the work-along, the API box, the Appendix D2 run flow and the breach cues, page 3 the two other demos, the polls, the lecture cards and your own prep. The flow is a suggested run of 130 instruction minutes plus the break: the page's core is 60 of them, and every other minute is an appendix insert, a demo or a lecture card you chose. Marks are elapsed minutes, not clock times. Nothing in this file addresses a learner.

Colour lanes in the PDF: blue = work the page (core); dashed blue = an appendix insert; red = you lecture, two minutes; green = a live demo off the page; teal = the API box; purple = a poll or a chat harvest; gold = the break; a black number = never skip; red text = stop by that mark.

## Pages 2 and 3: the live segments

**A · 0:32 · 10 min.** Live work-along: de-identify a client transcript. Real transcript on screen: not allowed. Synthetic or cleaned off-screen, then a personal account: allowed with conditions.
1. **Say why the transcript is synthetic.** A class of advisers from other firms is a nonaffiliated third party under Reg S-P section 248.10 and no exception fits (248.13 needs a service contract, 248.14 a client transaction, 248.15 the client's specific consent); CFP Standard A.9 has no teaching exception; a Zoom recording makes the share permanent. Read those sections yourself first. "So this is the Cole transcript, and the real one never leaves my firm."
2. **Tab 3.** The six turns of the Cole meeting in a Workspace Google Doc. Read one aloud. ASK "What here points to a person, or was told in confidence?" (Her mother's community, the graduation, David's division, Emma's card debt, the form that names his mother.)

Take the two confidences out as whole sentences; nothing replaces them (CASE.md Part J). Keep every fact the advice turns on.
4. **The banker test.** "Could a Naperville banker name this family from what is left?" If yes, replace more. This is Appendix D1 in one question.
5. **Tab 4.** Personal Claude, Privacy settings on screen: the training switch is **off**. Say the numbers: up to five years if it is on, 30 days if it is off (FACTS:claude-consumer-retention), and say no default. "This is plan A on the Appendix D2 map. My firm signed nothing."

**G · 1:01 · §04 cues.** The two clocks, and your story.
- **Black and white, said once:** the service provider tells the firm within 72 hours; the firm tells each affected client as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days after it becomes aware, counted from the day it knew, not the day counsel confirmed (FACTS:reg-sp-compliance). The rule sets no clock for telling a chief compliance officer: that window is the firm's own choice. In force for every covered firm: 3 December 2025 for larger entities, 3 June 2026 for smaller ones.
- **Two clocks, two starts.** The vendor's 72 hours run from the vendor's awareness; the firm's 30 days run from the firm's. The vendor does not owe the 72 hours on its own: the firm's policies and its contract make it owe them.
- **The key to read aloud:** event 2, the log read, is the defensible start. Counsel's letter (event 4) feels careful and lands six days late. The reading of "aware" is the lesson's, not a holding.
- **Off the page, optional:** Equifax knew on 29 July 2017 and told the public on 7 September: 40 days. Capital One determined an intrusion on 19 July 2019 and told the public on 29 July: ten days. Neither was under Reg S-P; the principle, count from awareness, is what they show. From the Fall 2026 build, several publishers each; the page no longer carries them.

**E · Lecture cards.** Two minutes each. Tie the page back to planning.
- **E1, after the bridge.** The four stops are the four questions your CCO, an examiner and a plaintiff's lawyer all ask about any deliverable: what went in, where it went, how you checked it, what you kept. A plan, a Roth analysis, a client letter: same four. The tool never changes the questions.
- **E2, after §02.** Nothing new binds you and everything old does. Reg S-P is why the prompt is a disclosure; the 1940 Act is why the advice must have a basis; FINRA 3110 is why your assistant's work is supervised work; CFP A.9 and A.14 are yours personally. Your firm's AI policy is a supervision document.
- **E3, after Appendix D3.** Approved means on paper. You already do this for custodians and software: the contract, the SOC 2 report, the breach clause. An officer with authority signs, never the adviser with a credit card. Once signed, the vendor is your service provider under Reg S-P and owes the firm 72 hours, because your policies must require it (FACTS:reg-sp-compliance); Anthropic's addendum promises 48.
- **E4, after §05.** The record is the plan file. Rule 204-2 already makes you keep the communication; the four slots are what make it re-checkable. A summary of a clause is your reading, not the clause. Complete is not correct.
