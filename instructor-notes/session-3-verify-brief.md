# The two recording-law items: what to open, what to look for, and what to say when you open C8

Rewritten on 2026-10-13 for the 2027 term. Appendix C8, "Recording Consent and the Confidentiality Obligation" (`s12`, 4 min, standard, after `sOff`), carries two items that a model must not sign and you must: the federal one-party floor and the duty of confidentiality applied to a documentation vendor. No text on the page addresses the instructor, so the items live here and in `session-3.md` under Check before teaching. Both primary sources were egress-blocked from the build environment when this was written (law.cornell.edu and cfp.net answered 403 on 2026-10-13), so nothing below is quoted; this is the shortest path to your own reading. With the targets named it should take ten minutes.

## Item 1 · 18 U.S.C. section 2511 as a federal one-party-consent floor

**Open:** 18 U.S.C. section 2511, subsection (2)(d). Cornell LII or uscode.house.gov.

**Look for:** the sentence beginning "It shall not be unlawful under this chapter for a person not acting under color of law to intercept a wire, oral, or electronic communication where such person is a party to the communication or where one of the parties to the communication has given prior consent", and its exception for interceptions made for a criminal or tortious purpose.

**What the page claims:** federal law lets one party record (SOURCES `src-wiretap`, H); some states require every party's consent and published counts disagree, so the page adopts no count. The footer record also cites Cal. Penal Code section 637.2, and the page does not state California's all-party rule as a number.

**Sign if:** (2)(d) says what the page says. **Strike if:** you read it as narrower than one-party consent for a private person recording her own call. Confidence before you read it: high, on the record's prior verification; the item is on the run sheet because the rule is that you sign recording law, not a model.

## Item 2 · The duty of confidentiality applied to a documentation vendor

**Open:** CFP Board, *Code of Ethics and Standards of Conduct*, Standard A.9, Confidentiality and Privacy (medium confidence on the letter and number from memory; find "Confidentiality and Privacy" in the Standards' table of contents). Then your firm's written policy on third-party vendors.

**Look for:** the duty to keep client information confidential; the permitted disclosures, which the Standard lists (ordinarily including disclosures the client consents to, disclosures to service providers who need the information to serve the client under a confidentiality obligation, and disclosures required by law); and the separate duty to protect client information and to adopt policies for it.

**What the page claims:** a vendor's system is a disclosure to everyone with access to it, so where the file lives, who can open it, how long it is kept and whether it trains a model are contract questions rather than preferences. C8 does not say the disclosure is prohibited; it says it is governed.

**Sign if:** the Standard treats a service provider as a permitted recipient under an obligation of confidentiality, which is what makes the contract questions load-bearing. **Strike or reword if:** your firm's policy forbids the disclosure outright, in which case the confidentiality bullet in C8 should say so for your room.
