## Item 1 · 18 U.S.C. § 2511 as a federal one-party-consent floor

**Open:** 18 U.S.C. § 2511, subsection (2)(d). Cornell LII or uscode.house.gov.

**Look for:** the sentence beginning "It shall not be unlawful under this chapter for a person not
acting under color of law to intercept a wire, oral, or electronic communication where such person
is a party to the communication or where one of the parties to the communication has given prior
consent", and its exception for interceptions made for a criminal or tortious purpose.

**What the page claims:** federal law lets one party record; some states require every party's
consent; the page adopts no count of those states. The footer record `src-wiretap` also cites Cal.
Penal Code § 637.2 and the lesson does not state California's all-party rule as a number.

**Sign if:** (2)(d) says what the page says. **Strike if:** you read it as narrower than one-party
consent for a private person recording her own call. Confidence before you read it: high, on the
audits' and the record's prior verification; the block exists because the rule is that you sign
recording law, not a model.

## Item 2 · The duty of confidentiality applied to a documentation vendor

**Open:** CFP Board, *Code of Ethics and Standards of Conduct*, Standard A.9, Confidentiality and
Privacy (medium confidence on the letter and number from memory; find "Confidentiality and Privacy"
in the Standards' table of contents). Then your firm's written policy on third-party vendors.

**Look for:** the duty to keep client information confidential; the permitted disclosures, which
the Standard lists (ordinarily including disclosures the client consents to, disclosures to
service providers who need the information to serve the client under a confidentiality
obligation, and disclosures required by law); and the separate duty to protect client information
and to adopt policies for it.

**What the page claims:** a vendor's system is a disclosure to everyone with access to it, so
where the file lives, who can open it, how long it is kept and whether it trains a model are
contract questions rather than preferences. §09 does not say the disclosure is prohibited; it
says it is governed.

**Sign if:** the Standard treats a service provider as a permitted recipient under an obligation
of confidentiality, which is what makes the contract questions load-bearing. **Strike or reword
if:** your firm's policy forbids the disclosure outright, in which case the second bullet in §09
should say so for your room.
