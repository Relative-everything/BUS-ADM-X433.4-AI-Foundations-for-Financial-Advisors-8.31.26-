## Named discussion: predicted split
- Prediction, not data, written for about eight learners: first vote 5 "I check more", 2 "I check the same", 1 "I check less" (about 63% on "more"). The majority reads a citation as a handle to pull. "Less" is the smallest group and speaks first, then "the same". At that size name the choice and the names, not a mean.
- Complication on screen, Phase three: the complication card in the page's defence panel. In `s4` the 2024 memo (dated 3 October 2024) ranked first at 42.6 against 24.8 for the 2026 plan description, a margin of 41.9%; the memo advised converting $150,000 on the premise that the Dunmore plan does not permit in-plan conversion, and that premise ended on 1 January 2026. It was retrieved accurately and quoted accurately, and the current document ranked second and was never the passage the model answered from. Opening the citation and confirming the passage says what the model said would not have caught it; only knowing which document should have been there would.
- Second vote: expect one or two voters to move from "more" to "the same", because more checking of the same kind would not have helped; expect nobody to move to "less". The finding is the sentence that moved them, not the delta. A zero delta is a finding; say so and stop.
