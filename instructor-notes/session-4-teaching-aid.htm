G · 1:01 · §04 cuesThe two clocks, and your story

  • Black and white, said once: the service provider tells the firm within 72 hours; the firm tells each affected client as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days after it becomes aware, counted from the day it knew, not the day counsel confirmed M. The rule sets no clock for telling a chief compliance officer: that window is the firm's own choice. In force for every covered firm: 3 December 2025 for larger entities, 3 June 2026 for smaller ones.
  • Two clocks, two starts. The vendor's 72 hours run from the vendor's awareness; the firm's 30 days run from the firm's. The vendor does not owe the 72 hours on its own: the firm's policies and its contract make it owe them.
  • The key to read aloud: event 2, the log read, is the defensible start. Counsel's letter (event 4) feels careful and lands six days late. The reading of "aware" is the lesson's, not a holding.
  • Off the page, optional: Equifax knew on 29 July 2017 and told the public on 7 September: 40 days. Capital One determined an intrusion on 19 July 2019 and told the public on 29 July: ten days. Neither was under Reg S-P; the principle, count from awareness, is what they show. From the Fall 2026 build, several publishers each; the page no longer carries them.
