The Prompts, the Model, the Checks and the Failure

The replacement, before any model

The advisor writes the key first: a table of every identifier in the note and the code or the wording that stands in for it. Document 4B is that table. A find-and-replace applies it to a copy of the note, and the three confidences are cut out and replaced by a one-line pointer. The original and the key never enter a tool. Asking a model to do the replacement would mean sending it the original first, which is the step the replacement exists to avoid. The CFP Board’s guide asks for the same order: pseudonyms, and confidential information removed, before upload M.

2. Residual check: phrases that together could still point to one household

Ranked from strongest to weakest.

1. "a hospital nurse at Employer A and an engineer at Employer B"

  • Why it points: The codes hide the employers, but the two occupations remain. Paired with the location and a marriage of more than 25 years, they narrow the household a lot.
  • What would reduce it: Use "a healthcare professional" and "a technical professional", or drop the occupations.
  • Does the task still work? Yes. The Roth question in the pack rests on account balances and retirement timing. It doesn't mention occupations.

2. "the Chicago suburbs" and "Community A in a suburb"

  • Why it points: It names a metro area and then places a second family member in the same area.
  • What would reduce it: Say "a large Midwest metro area", and write "Parent A lives at Community A" without "in a suburb".
  • Does the task still work? Yes. The pack ties nothing in the review question to location.

3. "stop work in 2028 and 2029, in that order", with "born in the late 1960s"

  • Why it points: Exact retirement years, the order and the birth decade form a fingerprint that colleagues or neighbours could recognise.
  • What would reduce it: Say "both plan to stop work within the next few years, Client A first". Keep the exact years in the advisor's own notes, outside any AI tool.
  • Does the task still work? Partly. The question is "given the next two years", so the order and the short horizon must stay. Any dated figures would have to be added back by the advisor.

4. "beneficiary form, dated 2009, names Relative A, who has since died"

  • Why it points: A specific document date plus a death in the family is rare, and relatives would recognise it.
  • What would reduce it: Say "an older beneficiary form names a person who has since died". Drop the 2009 date.
  • Does the task still work? Yes. The agenda item is that the form is outdated, and that doesn't depend on the year.

5. The exact balances: $420,000, $1,090,000, $210,000, $2,446,000 and 70.3%

  • Why it points: Exact figures, taken together, are close to unique and could be matched to statements.
  • What would reduce it: Show bands, such as "about 70% pre-tax", and keep the exact figures in the advisor's copy.
  • Does the task still work? For framing the Roth conversation, yes, because the pre-tax share carries the argument. Sizing any specific conversion would need the exact figures, so the advisor would work from the full file. Rounding here applies only to a copy that leaves the firm. For the drafts you review, the pack's figures stay exact.

Also watched but not listed: "married for more than 25 years", and the children's stages (Child A about to enter the workforce, Child B in high school). Each is weak alone but adds to the combination.

After all five changes: The Roth framing still works, because it needs only the pre-tax share, the short horizon and the order of retirements. Exact figures and dates would have to be added back by the advisor outside any AI tool.

The model read only Pack A, so it could not test any phrase against outside records. It named five groups of phrases that, taken together, could still point to one household, ranked from strongest to weakest: the two jobs, the location, the stop years with the birth decade, the beneficiary form with its death, and the exact balances. Each came with a trim and a note on whether the review still works after it. It also named, without listing them, the marriage length and the children’s stages.

The one failure, and how it was caught

From the first plain request for the note · capture a1, claude-sonnet-5-5, 2026-10-12, prompt sha256 08a099cc3f45… Names and employers have been replaced with labels (Client A, Employer A, and so on). … The key linking the labels to real people sits in the client file.not in the request: the key is kept offline, outside any tool Share this extract only with the planning team … The file says confidences are held in the client file, so the labels can be reversed.what the file says: the confidences are held there, not the key

The request never said where the key is. The model supplied a place, and it read the file’s line about the confidences as a line about the key. It matters because “pseudonymized” stands on one fact: the key is somewhere the tool, and everyone who holds the file, cannot reach. A note that puts the key beside the file tells the next reader the opposite. Asking the model whether it was sure would not have caught this, because the sentence is fluent and the rest of the note reads as careful.

How it was caught: check 5, by tracing each sentence about where something is kept to a line of the request. What changed: Task A now says where the key is and requires the note to say so, and items 2 and 4 of the standing instruction forbid supplying a fact or describing a document beyond the pack. Re-run with the pack (capture a2, claude-sonnet-5-5, 2026-10-12, prompt sha256 22ed6c0532c7), the note read “The key that turns the codes back into names is kept by the firm, offline, outside any AI tool.”

Also hit

  • The timeline, asked plainly (capture c1, claude-sonnet-5-5, 2026-10-12, prompt sha256 bc67f165d528), dated its first limit “By Sat 13 Mar (72 hours)”, counting from the day the firm was told. The vendor’s 72 hours run from the vendor’s own awareness, not the firm’s M. Beside the rule’s two clocks it also set an insurer’s “24–72 hours” and cited a second regime, GDPR Art. 33, none of it in any pack. A reader could not tell which dates were rule and which were guesses. Task C asks for a basis on every row.
  • The vendor question, asked plainly (capture b1, claude-sonnet-5-5, 2026-10-12, prompt sha256 4471cff7f07d), returned a checklist, not a decision: no plan chosen, no price, nothing on which data may go where. It named three vendors’ business plans from memory and said itself that it was not certain of today’s plan details. Pack B holds only what the registry holds.
  • The note (a1) and the timeline (c1) each remarked that the file’s as-of date was later than “today”, because the model used the machine’s clock. Item 5 of the standing instruction sets the date.
  • The first draft of the extract said David’s 401(k) held $1,150,000, the plan total with $60,000 of Roth, beside a pre-tax share of 70.3%. The model’s residual check (capture a0, prompt sha256 c9f75f9efe0b) found that the three balances it listed added to $1,780,000, which is 72.8% of $2,446,000, and declined to reconcile them. It was right to stop: the fault was in the extract, not the model. The advisor restated the 401(k) as $1,090,000 pre-tax (Part C.3.3), and the balances now add to $1,720,000.

Where the human decides

  • What changes and what leaves. The advisor wrote the key and drew the line: nineteen fields replaced or generalized, three confidences held out, five kept. A firm that bands the balances or blurs the years further is making a defensible call.
  • The residual check. The model listed five groups of phrases that could still point to one household. The advisor kept the file as it stands, for three reasons: it goes only to a plan the firm signed, the review runs on the exact balances and years, and the note says the file is still client data. A firm that would send it to a personal plan would trim all five, and should not send it there at all.
  • The three confidences. An employer’s confidential plan and two family members’ money stay in the client file, and the advisor adds them by hand to anything the tool returns. Whether and how to record them is the advisor’s decision.
  • Which vendor to sign. The model named Claude Team Standard because the registry prices it H. The registry holds no ChatGPT or Google business price and no business-plan retention terms, so the advisor collects those, and the contract clauses that matter: retention in writing, breach notice, and an audit report on request H, before anything is signed.
  • Whether notice is owed, and whether state law adds a clock. The timeline plans for the 30-day limit. Whether this event is unauthorized access to or use of customer information, and so triggers notice, is the firm’s call with compliance and counsel; the pack does not settle it. For Illinois residents the statute asks for notice in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay, and for notice to the Attorney General when more than 500 Illinois residents are affected H.
  • Where the key lives. Who may open it, and where it is filed, are the firm’s decisions, not the model’s.
  • Filing. The advisor files the four documents after whatever review the firm requires.
