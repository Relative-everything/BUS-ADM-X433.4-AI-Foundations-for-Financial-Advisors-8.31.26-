## 2. UI: one defect class found and fixed

`layout-audit.mjs` counts four kinds of layout defect in both versions and fails where the branch has more.

**Found.** Step 2 had snapped every font size onto the type scale, including sizes on text inside SVG drawings. Those sizes are in the drawing's own units and the labels were placed for them. Snapped up (9.5 to 12.5, 11 to 12.5, 13 to 14), labels overlapped and ran outside their figures: 18 labels outside a drawing (0 before) and 27 more overlapping pairs, on Session 5 (`#e2Fig`, `#s2Fig`, `#s2Meter`, `#s3Fig`, `#s4Fig`) and two labels on Session 2. This is the failure the handback's red team named and the screenshots I had sampled did not show.

**Fixed.** `restore-svg-sizes.mjs` asks each page, in the browser, what every size-setting rule matches. A rule that matches only SVG text had its size put back to the value it had on main: 294 rules (Session 2: 2, Session 4: 144, Session 5: 144, promoted block: 4). Nothing else in those rules changed, so they keep the new face, weight and colours. D-136.

**Left.** One element: on Session 5 at 390 px with everything revealed, one tile label in `#s3Inputs` measures 3 px taller than its box (3 such elements on main, 4 now). Registered under DW-214.

**Read by eye.** The full-length captures of Sessions 4 and 5 were tiled and read screen by screen (12 sheets), with samples of the others. No broken component found. One chart on Session 5 whose row labels cross its axis line was checked against main: identical before and after.

## 6. Second round, same day: every control on every page, used on both versions

Asked for by the instructor before merging: view each page, use each interaction, and show by software-testing practice that the UI update breaks nothing. The method is differential testing: the page before the restyle is the oracle, and the restyled page must do what it does.

### `interaction-audit.mjs`: behaviour, step for step

Both versions of a page are opened side by side. The same real input is played on both, one step at a time: every visible, enabled control gets a real pointer click (the browser refuses to click what is covered or off screen), text fields are typed into, sliders are moved with the arrow keys, selects are changed, then Shift+U, Tab, Escape and the arrow keys. Controls that a click creates are found on the next pass and used too. On a lesson the sweep runs twice, as loaded and with the appendix opened to its deepest tier, and the depth buttons are clicked last. After every step both pages are read (every text node, what is visible, how many controls are visible and enabled, every field's value, the class of every element inside a task, the element count, script errors) and must agree.

**What the test cannot do, said plainly.**
- 500 controls could not be reached by a pointer on either version at the moment the test tried them (covered by another element in both, or disabled in both). 424 of them were still exercised through the element's own click and compared; the rest were disabled on both.
- Session 3's turning cube (`#cubeWrap`, `#cubeOut`) redraws frame by frame in real time and reports the on-screen distance at that instant, so its interior is left out of the text and class comparison. Its buttons are still clicked and compared.
- Dragging is not tested. Every sorter was used by click (pick an item, pick a bucket).
- The clipboard is replaced by one that always succeeds, on both versions, because a headless browser refuses the real one.
- Links that leave the page are compared by address and not followed.
- Fallback fonts, as everywhere in this audit.

Four faults turned up in the test itself while it was built, none in the pages: a stepped clock that stopped the frames the browser waits on before clicking; markers on elements that components rebuild on every click; a click at the midpoint of an SVG word group, which is empty space; and comparing a "Copy" button during its one-second "Copied" state. Each is fixed in the tool and described in its header.
