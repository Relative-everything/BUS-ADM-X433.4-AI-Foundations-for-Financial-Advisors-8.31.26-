Retirement clocksMeg's applicable age is 75; her first distribution year is 2037. The inherited IRA runs a separate ten-year clock to 31 December 2031 with annual RMDs, because Walter Hensley died after his required beginning date. The 2026 RMD is $43,290.

The proposed structure, in five lines. Nothing in it has been executed.

  1. Meg contributes all CPC stock to Cole Family Holdings LLC, recapitalized into 100 voting and 900 non-voting units of 1,000. Economic rights per unit are identical; only the vote differs.
  2. A 30% combined lack-of-control and lack-of-marketability discount is asserted on the non-voting units, putting them at $38,500 each against $55,000 for a voting unit. No appraiser has been engaged and the memorandum cites no study.
  3. She gifts 52 non-voting units, $2,002,000, to the Cole 2026 Irrevocable Trust, a SLAT for David. That consumes $2,002,000 of her $15,000,000 exclusion, leaving $12,998,000.
  4. She sells 520 more units, $20,020,000, to the same trust for a demand note at 3.82%, interest only, $764,764 a year. Seed is exactly 10.00% of the note. Rev. Rul. 85-13 disregards the sale for income tax.
  5. The trust applies dividends above note interest to buy Meg's remaining 328 units in annual tranches, reaching all 900 by the end of year 5 for $12,628,000 of cash.
Where it landsMeg after closing: $5,500,000 of voting units + $12,628,000 of non-voting + $20,020,000 note = $38,148,000, down $16,852,000 from $55,000,000. That reduction has two components: $11,440,000 of below-pro-rata transfer plus $5,412,000 of discount on the 328 units she kept. The trust holds $22,022,000 of units against the $20,020,000 note: net equity $2,002,000, equal to the seed gift exactly.
The cashThe LLC distributes pro rata, so Meg draws on all 428 units she holds after closing: $2,140,000 in year 1, not $500,000. In years 1 through 4 the entire $5,000,000 round-trips to her. From year 6 she holds only the voting block: $1,264,764 in against $1,786,850 of tax and household gap, a shortfall of $522,086 a year.
The burnWhile grantor-trust status holds, Meg reports 100% of the LLC's income whatever the trust owns. The tax she pays on the trust's share is a transfer of value to the trust that consumes no exemption and is not a gift (Rev. Rul. 2004-64). $822,250 in year 1, $1,293,750 a year at steady state, $30,876,930 cumulative by Meg's age 89. It is the mechanism, not a cost.
Documents2011 wills and a joint revocable trust with a credit-shelter formula written for a $5,000,000 exclusion and no Illinois QTIP direction · a 2014 buy-sell, never amended, whose formula produces about $18,000,000 against a $55,000,000 indicated value · a 2023 appraisal concluding $38,000,000 with no discount study · unexecuted 2026 drafts of the trust and note · no LLC agreement, no 2026 valuation, no life insurance, no Form 709 ever filed.
CharitableNo donor-advised fund has ever existed. $80,000 a year in direct cash to four public charities. Meg chairs the Rockford Workforce Alliance and intends a $5,000,000 lifetime endowment to it. Undecided, unpledged, and the board has not been told.

Open in the file, and answered by no session.

  • Which rate governs the note. §7872's blended annual rate at 3.82%, which the memorandum uses, or §1274's AFR for a note issued for property — 4.10% if a demand note is treated as short-term, which the statute does not say. On that reading the difference is $56,056 of interest a year. No controlling authority resolves it.
  • Whether the voting block carries a premium. The memorandum values 100 units holding sole voting control at pro-rata value with no adjustment.
  • Whether Meg's retained non-voting units carry a discount while she controls the LLC through the voting block. $5,412,000 of the estate reduction depends on the answer.
  • Whether discretionary distributions survive §2036(a)(2). Mirowski turned on mandatory distributions; the draft operating agreement makes them discretionary.
  • Whether the Illinois exposure is addressed at all. Neither spouse has engaged with it, and the 2011 documents contain no Illinois QTIP direction.

The question that runs through every session

Meg is short $522,086 a year from year 6, while the structure performs exactly as designed. How much of the $20,020,000 note does she call this year, what does calling it cost her in every year after, and when is a different lever the better answer?

No session answers it. Every session works on a different part of it. The arithmetic is in the case: each $1,000,000 called permanently removes $38,200 of future interest, so the gap widens by 3.82% of every call. Nothing in the case says whether the structure is advisable, and neither does this course.
