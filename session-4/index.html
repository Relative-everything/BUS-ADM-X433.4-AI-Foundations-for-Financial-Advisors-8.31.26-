Appendix D1 · Provenance16 min

Content Provenance and the SynthID Watermark

You cannot detect a deepfake by looking at it. Provenance marking is the answer, and it answers a narrower question than most people think.

Two approaches. Metadata bindings attach a signed manifest alongside the content — C2PA Content Credentials. In-content watermarks alter the content itself. SynthID is the second kind, and metadata is separable: manifests are lost when a non-aware tool resaves a file, re-encoding strips them silently, and a screenshot defeats them.H

A correction to the common framing

SynthID and C2PA are routinely compared as rivals. The specification says otherwise: C2PA defines a Durable Content Credential as one for which soft bindings exist — invisible watermarks and perceptual fingerprints letting a stripped manifest be recovered. Watermarking is a component of the C2PA architecture, not its competitor.H If asked to choose between them, the question is malformed.

The asymmetry that governs everything else

Presence is evidence. Absence is not.

A valid watermark tells you something. Its absence tells you almost nothing — the content may predate adoption, the model may not participate, the file may have been stripped. Most authentic content carries no marking, so treating absence as suspicion produces an unusable false-positive rate. [UNCONFIRMED]H Anthropic has not announced SynthID adoption for Claude, so nothing you generate in this course is watermarked. [UNCONFIRMED]M

Text: tournament sampling

SynthID adds nothing to the text. It changes which token is selected, running a tournament in which each candidate carries a pseudo-random value seeded by recent context plus a secret key. Detection re-runs the same tournaments: if observed tokens win their brackets far more often than chance allows, the text is watermarked — no access to the original model needed.H

Entropy, Regeneration and the Limits of Watermarking

Watermark capacity is bounded by how many acceptable outputs exist. Where only one output is correct, there is nowhere to hide a signal.

Google states it directly: watermark application is less effective on factual responses, because there is less opportunity to augment generation without decreasing accuracy.H

The advisor's inversion

The outputs where fabrication does most damage — the discount percentage, an IRC cite, the note balance — are the lowest-entropy outputs and the least watermarkable. What watermarks well is what matters least when wrong.

Code and structured data are the extreme case. Change a token and it compiles differently or fails. The remaining slack sits in naming, quoting and whitespace — and a formatter reprints to a fixed standard, erasing exactly that. Nobody has to attack the watermark; the build pipeline removes it.M

Regeneration

Passing a watermarked image through a second generative model re-synthesises it from a model with no knowledge of the key. Zhao et al. (2024) formalised this as a regeneration attack; a 2026 analysis proves mutual information between image and payload approaches zero under sufficient diffusion, with near-zero recovery at preserved visual fidelity.H

Not uniformly, and the nuance is the useful part. Schemes binding the signal to latent representations rather than pixels degrade far less — one 2026 scheme retains 78.8% bit accuracy and 75% true-positive rate after diffusion purification. [UNCONFIRMED]M Treat any single removal percentage as scheme-specific until proven otherwise.

This session does not teach removal technique and none is given here. The finding matters defensively, and only defensively: it is the reason a compliance procedure must never treat a missing watermark as evidence of authenticity. Sources: Zhao et al. (2024) on regeneration attacks; arXiv 2602.20680 (Feb 2026) for the information-theoretic result; arXiv 2605.01479 for the semantic-binding counterexample. Christ et al. further prove that watermark undetectability requires cryptographic secrecy, so no scheme is secure against an adversary who holds the key.H

Hallucination Rates and the Verification Burden

Session 1: why it happens. Session 3: how often. Tonight: what it costs you.

You have the mechanism and you have the rate. Neither has yet been converted into minutes. That conversion is the only form in which this argument survives contact with a practice.

The rate evidence is Magesh, Surani, Dahl, Suzgun, Manning and Ho, Hallucination-Free?, Stanford RegLab and HAI; preprint May 2024, published in the Journal of Empirical Legal Studies 2025. Over 200 preregistered legal queries, expert hand-scored. LexisNexis had marketed "100% hallucination-free linked legal citations."H

Read the reduction honestly. Ungrounded to best grounded: 43% to 17%, a 60% relative cut; to the worse grounded tool, 43% to 33%, a 23% cut. Grounding is worth doing and is not a solution. The higher rate also correlated with longer responses — roughly 350 words against 219 — so the more thorough-looking answer was likelier to contain something false. [NEEDS SOURCE]M

A measurement you cannot put on the same axis

Artificial Analysis currently measures Claude Opus 5 at a 50% hallucination rate, and places Claude Fable 5 top of the boards tracking how often a model is simply wrong, at 40 on AA-Omniscience.H Do not put that 50% next to the Stanford 43%. Different instruments, query sets and scoring rules, two years apart. Both find double-digit error rates on their own terms; neither licenses a claim about the other.

The arithmetic of not checking

If a tool errs on proportion p of citations and you verify none of N, the probability at least one error reaches your recommendation is 1 − (1 − p)N.
