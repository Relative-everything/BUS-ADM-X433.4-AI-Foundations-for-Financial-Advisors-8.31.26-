02 · Client data6 min

What Counts as Client Data

Before anything goes into an AI tool, ask: would this tell someone who the client is, or that they are your client? Regulation S-P calls that nonpublic personal information, NPI.

  • NPI, in plain words. Financial information you hold about a client that is not public. The fact that someone is your client counts on its own H.
  • Public facts can become NPI. A company's name is public. Written into a prompt about your client, it is disclosed in a way that shows she is your client H.
  • You met the next one in Session 3. "Sole owner of a Rockford aerospace-fastener maker" names nobody and still points to one person. Regulation S-P is why that is more than a hunch: what it protects is information that identifies the client, whether or not a name appears M, applied.
  • The CFP Board's advice. Replace names with pseudonyms and strip identifying details before you upload anything M.

The Thirty-Day Clock: A Regulation S-P Tabletop

Regulation S-P gives a firm 30 days to tell affected clients about a breach, counted from when the firm becomes aware of it. The hard word is aware.

  • The rule. Notify affected clients as soon as practicable, and no later than 30 days after becoming aware that their sensitive information was, or is reasonably likely to have been, reached without authorisation H.
  • The gap. A policy that never says whose knowledge counts as the firm's leaves the start date to argument.
  • The fix is one clause. Name the roles that must escalate, the hours they have, and who owns the date.

Replace Details, Don't Just Delete Them

Deleting details can wreck a prompt. Replacing them keeps it useful. The skill is a replacement that tells the model what it needs and tells nobody who the client is.

  • Swap names for roles. "Meg Cole" becomes "the client"; "Nathan" becomes "her son".
  • Round, or give a range. An exact value becomes "tens of millions".
  • Widen the place and the trade. "A Rockford aerospace-fastener maker" becomes "a Midwest manufacturer".
  • Never ask the AI to anonymise it for you. Pasting the real text to get it cleaned has already sent it.
  • Keep the key offline. The note that maps "the client" back to a name stays in your own files, never in a prompt.

Appendix D1 · Watermarks14 min

How a Text Watermark Works

Text from new Claude models now carries an invisible watermark. What it is, who can read it, and what it says about the person who used it.

  • What changed. Anthropic marks text from supported Claude models launched from 2 August 2026, and says every model will be covered by 2 December 2026. Image files Claude makes carry signed provenance data, called Content Credentials H.
  • Who can check. Only organisations that EU law makes eligible, such as regulators, researchers and the press. You cannot run the check on a document yourself H.
  • What a mark says. That the text may have passed through Claude. Nothing on Anthropic's page says it identifies the account or the person who used it M.
  • How it works. The model picks each word from a short list of likely candidates. The watermark runs a tournament among them, scored with a secret key. The words still read naturally; a detector holding the key sees that the same kind of winner keeps winning M, via the paper it names.

What a Watermark Can and Cannot Prove

A mark is evidence. No mark is not evidence of anything.

  • Absence proves nothing. Anthropic's own page says a missing mark does not mean the text was not made or touched by AI H.
  • Rewriting removes it. Heavy paraphrase or translation can make the mark undetectable H.
  • Facts carry it worst. Google says watermarking works less well on factual answers, where there are fewer good ways to say the same thing H. The outputs that hurt most when wrong, a discount rate, a Code section, a balance, are the ones a mark fits least.
