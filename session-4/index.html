The proposed structure, in five lines. Nothing in it has been executed.

  1. Meg contributes all CPC stock to Cole Family Holdings LLC, recapitalized into 100 voting and 900 non-voting units of 1,000. Economic rights per unit are identical; only the vote differs.
  2. A 30% combined lack-of-control and lack-of-marketability discount is asserted on the non-voting units, putting them at $38,500 each against $55,000 for a voting unit. No appraiser has been engaged and the memorandum cites no study.
  3. She gifts 52 non-voting units, $2,002,000, to the Cole 2026 Irrevocable Trust, a SLAT for David. That consumes $2,002,000 of her $15,000,000 exclusion, leaving $12,998,000.
  4. She sells 520 more units, $20,020,000, to the same trust for a demand note at 3.82%, interest only, $764,764 a year. Seed is exactly 10.00% of the note. Rev. Rul. 85-13 disregards the sale for income tax.

The question that runs through every session

Meg is short $522,086 a year from year 6, while the structure performs exactly as designed. How much of the $20,020,000 note does she call this year, what does calling it cost her in every year after, and when is a different lever the better answer?

      Discussion: Does the Record Cost More Than the Tool Saves?

      This session adds work to every AI task: clean the prompt, vet the tool, check the answers, keep the record. Put that work on a scale against the time AI saves.

      Do this now: 15 minutes

      Vote, Weigh Both Cases, Add the Complication, Vote Again

      The proposition. For a typical recurring deliverable, the full burden costs more time than the AI saves.

      1 · Vote. It cannot be changed.

      ◀ burden winssaving wins ▶
      Weigh:
      Deliverables sharing the setup
      12 = one a month for a year
      Answers to check

      Setup minutes, sharing counts and the ×2 and ×4 what-ifs are illustrative; the rest are your §06 numbers (its starting values if you left them)

      Your first votePick a, b, c or d above. It cannot be changed, and it loads the scale.
      The case for agreeingChecking grows with every answer and never gets faster. Add vetting the tool, the placeholders and the record block, and one deliverable can cost more than it saves.
      The case for disagreeingDrafting is where the time goes, and AI cuts it to a fraction. A short client email has few claims to check, so the saving pays for the checking.
      The complicationSetup is paid once per workflow, not once per deliverable: the tool is vetted once, the placeholders and the record block are set up once. Checking and filling in the record come back every time.

      4 · Vote again

      Your second voteVote again after the complication.
      • Setup is paid once. Vetting the tool, the placeholders and the record block are shared by every deliverable that reuses them.
      • Checking is paid every time. So is filling in the record. Neither spreads, so however often you reuse the setup, you never keep more than your §06 figure.
      • Your numbers decide it. Try ×4 at 12: enough answers to check can outweigh any amount of sharing.
